Bring sample covariation and spatial neighborhoods into module learning

Cellular ecosystems have two forms of organization: populations vary together across samples, and their activities form local patterns within tissue. CoVarNet provides a composition-based coordinate system for the first. Connecting these levels calls for spatial neighborhoods to participate in module learning.

SpaCoVarNet-GS connects sample compositions and spatial observations through a shared module basis, retains view-specific activities, and organizes them with graph constraints and explicit sparsity.Spatial coordinates enter the optimization objective alongside cross-sample composition.

In the historical 106-state experiment, the real spatial graph produced lower activity roughness than both coordinate-permuted and degree-preserving rewired controls in all 30 Frobenius initializations and all 15 KL initializations.That consistent direction on a fixed input is the central quantitative result.

Scope: the August 2026 reference with 755 single-cell samples, one 6,090-cell CosMx field and rank 9. Initializations evaluate algorithmic repetitions on the same data; all activities are evaluated on the same real spatial graph.

02 /

A shared basis captures composition while each view retains its distribution

The model operates on a common cell-state axis. Each column of W describes a module's state composition; each column of H describes module activity in a sample or spatial observation:

X_sc ≈ W H_sc X_st ≈ W H_st W: states × modules; H_v: modules × observations

Sharing W creates a common module coordinate system. Separate H_sc and H_st preserve each view's module mixtures, allowing an observation to express several modules through continuous activities.

Reconstruction weights are set by observation count, with an additional 0.1 multiplier for CosMx. This makes each view's contribution explicit. Views align on state identifiers and row order; the CosMx soft mapping uses its 486 covered reference genes.

The CoVarNet nsNMF baseline uses X≈WSH. SpaCoVarNet-GS explicitly specifies graph and L1 terms, making neighborhood organization and sparsity separately controlled modeling choices. CoVarNet study · nsNMF model definition

Real neighborhoods give a consistent activity response across losses

The graph term asks whether neighboring observations can form a more coherent module-activity representation. For affinity A, weighted-degree matrix D and L=D−A, Tr(HLHᵀ) incorporates local relationships into activity learning.

Coordinate permutation changes the correspondence between observations and geometry. Degree-preserving rewiring changes connection partners while retaining node degrees and the global edge-weight multiset. Activities from all training configurations are evaluated on the same real graph.

04 /

Give reconstruction graph structure and sparsity distinct roles

The objective separates three modeling needs: reconstruction retains observed information, graph terms organize neighborhood activities, and L1 terms regularize basis and activity distributions. The solver also exposes an optional factor-dissimilarity term:

min W,H_v ≥ 0 Σ_v ω_v D(X_v || W H_v) + ½ Σ_v λ_v Tr(H_v L_v H_vᵀ) + α ||W||₁ + β Σ_v ||H_v||₁ + ½ δ ||offdiag(WᵀW)||²_F

The reported experiments use δ=0 and focus on graph constraints under two reconstruction criteria. Frobenius and generalized KL each have their own reconstruction updates; graph and L1 terms enter through shared optimization interfaces. The KL branch updates W, H and objective tracking consistently.

R = X / max(WH, ε) H ← H ⊙ [ω WᵀR + λ H A] / [ω Wᵀ1 + λ H D + β + ε]

cKDTree builds local neighbors and sparse graph arithmetic avoids storing a full pairwise-distance matrix. Fixed-basis projection then estimates activities for a new region in the learned coordinate system. Interfaces, matrix scale and algorithm versions are documented in the methods supplement.

05 /

Match each experiment to activity organization or dictionary reconstruction

Neighborhood controls evaluate local organization in H. Fixed-W regional projection evaluates how the shared basis explains a new region's observed state composition. Reading these outputs separately shows how graph constraints affect representation structure and reconstruction.

Two coordinate partitions of the same field each produced eight folds, with entire blocks excluded from training and training graphs rebuilt. Real-graph mean MSE was lower than for both null graphs in both partitions. Relative to disabling graph penalties in both views, mean MSE increased by 2.84×10⁻⁵ and 1.42×10⁻⁶. Block-level directions vary, describing the fitting tradeoff under this fixed configuration.

Projection estimates H from observed regional X with W fixed. The no-graph configuration disables both sc and spatial graph terms. The complete regional comparisons retain every control, both error measures and historical tests.

06 /

Spatial neighborhoods become part of module learning

SpaCoVarNet-GS connects sample compositions and spatial observations with a shared basis, organizes activities through graph constraints, and controls decomposition structure with explicit penalties.In the historical 106-state experiment, real-graph roughness was lower than for both null controls in every initialization under both losses.

The framework gives each part a clear role: W expresses common composition, H expresses view-specific activity, and neighborhoods enter the learning objective. Cross-sample covariation and local spatial arrangement become components of one interpretable factorization.
